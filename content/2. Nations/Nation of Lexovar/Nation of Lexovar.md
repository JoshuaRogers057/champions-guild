*Gnomes of Eryndor*

## Government

The Nation of Lexovar is ruled by the **Arcane Council**, a five-member body of scholars, engineers, arcanists, and civic specialists.

The council is technocratic by design. Its central belief is simple: the people who understand a problem best should be the ones trusted to solve it. Council seats are not inherited. They are elected by the four great magic academies of Lexovar. Electorates can be selected based on their inventions, academic service, or recognized expertise.

The Arcane Council has five seats. Four belong to representatives from the four great academy cities. The fifth belongs to the Chancellor of Ciliren, who serves as chair of the council and national leader. The four academy seats represent Lexovar's greatest fields of expertise:

| Academy | Specialty |
|---|---|
| Fizzleforge University | Experimental magitech and construct design |
| Gearshield Academy | Engineering, infrastructure, and combat suit design |
| Institute of Scholastic Innovation and Sciences | Chronomancy and magical theory |
| Gleamspire Academy | Spell research, archives, and arcane classification |

Ciliren has five smaller academies. They do not each hold a council seat. Instead, they oversee the election of the Chancellor of Ciliren. This compromise keeps the academy cities from being dominated by the capital. It also keeps Ciliren at the center of national power.

In theory, Lexovar is ruled by reason and expertise. In practice, every expert belongs to a school, and every school has rivals. Policy fights often escalate quickly at the national level. Lexovar works because its leaders are brilliant. It struggles because brilliance does not remove pride.

## Military

Lexovar has the smallest standing army among the major nations, but by far the most advanced in Eryndor. Lexovar does not win wars with numbers. They win with constructs, fragment weapons, and battlefield engineering.

Constructs form the first line of defense. They guard cities, patrol rail stations, protect vaults, and absorb the danger gnomes prefer not to waste lives on. They don't eat, drink, or sleep; their deployment can be nearly instantaneous.

Lexovar does not field soldiers in the traditional sense. It fields skilled operators, trained to deploy a wide range of magitech to accomplish the task before them. To the Lexovari mind, one specialist with the right devices at their disposal is worth a dozen conventional soldiers.

Lexovar does not project power through marching armies or conquered territory. Its influence spreads through dependency. Other nations rely on gnomish inventions to move goods, increase production in mines and farms, and modernize infrastructure. Lexovar's strength is not the threat of invasion. It is the quiet understanding that much of modern Eryndor works better when Lexovar keeps the machines running.

## Economy

The Gnomish economy revolves around its magic schools and its inventors and engineers, pioneers of Magitech and innovation.

Lexovari magitech is sold across Eryndor to any settlement wealthy enough to afford it. Farming villages use magitech pumps and plows. Mining towns hire Lexovari technicians and drill crews. Cities commission civic machines. Guilds purchase specialized tools. Nobles pay for construct support when labor or security becomes too costly.

The rail system is Lexovar's greatest economic triumph. It binds distant nations together through routes Lexovar designed, expanded, and still profits from maintaining. Ciliren may not be the trade capital of Eryndor, but every freight car that crosses a foreign border, every passenger line that opens a new market, and every nation that leans on the rails sends coin back toward Lexovar.

Though Ciliren is not Eryndor's trade capital, the continent's growing infrastructure increasingly bends in its direction. Travelers pass through its stations, foreign powers negotiate around Lexovari systems, and distant markets depend on routes its engineers made possible. The more Eryndor modernizes, the more wealth flows back to Lexovar.

## Fragment Access

Lexovar has brilliant magitech, but poor natural fragment access.

The nation controls only a few minor fragment mines, and those mines cannot sustain its inventions. Lexovar understands fragments better than nearly anyone, but it does not possess enough of them.

Because of this, Lexovar buys fragments wherever it can. Its largest suppliers are Kar Dromm, Thundrakar, and the Redmarch Coalition, but gnomish buyers will negotiate with almost anyone willing to sell. Quiet black-market purchases and negotiations in academic halls are not uncommon and help keep the nation running.

This dependence is Lexovar's greatest weakness. If the flow of fragments slows, the rail suffers first. Then the constructs. Then the academies. Lexovar is powerful because the world needs its machines, but Lexovar survives because the world keeps selling it the fuel.

## Culture

Lexovar culture is built on utility, bureaucracy, and school loyalty.

A thing is respected when it works. In Lexovar a person is respected when they make something work, or make it work better. Beauty has value, but usefulness has more. A bridge may be elegant, but it had better hold.

Nothing truly exists in Lexovar until it has been documented, approved, and peer reviewed. An invention may function perfectly in a workshop, but it is not considered real until the proper institutions recognize it. Citizens grumble about the delays, but most believe the process protects them. In Lexovar, bureaucracy is not the enemy of genius. It is the cage that keeps genius from burning down the street.

Academy loyalty runs deep. A gnome's school often matters like a hometown, family crest, or military regiment matters to other nations. Graduates wear school colors, follow old rivalries, fund new research, and defend their academy long after leaving it. This makes Lexovar brilliant, organized, and competitive. It also makes the nation status-conscious. A citizen without credentials may be ignored, and an invention without approval may as well not exist.

### Holidays

**The Grand Showing** — January 1
A raucous national festival. Cities host invention fairs, public demonstrations, and friendly competitions. Crowds cheer both success and spectacular failure.

**Patent Day** — April 11
A civic celebration of law and innovation. Contracts are announced publicly, guilds host banquets, and apprentices receive ceremonial tools.

**Malfunction Remembrance** — August 26
A lighthearted remembrance turned celebration. Stories of famous failures are retold with humor, followed by fireworks and safety parades.

## Religion

Gnomes nearly universally worship **Gond, the Wonderbringer** — god of invention, craft, and knowledge.

**Influence:** Gond inspires relentless creativity and experimentation.

**Festivals:** The Festival of Gond, held each solstice, includes invention competitions across every imaginable category.

## Mercenaries & Criminal Organizations

### The Brass Wardens

*Home Nation: Lexovar | Honor Scale: 8/10*
*Symbol: A brass shield with a black gear*
*Leader: Marshal Tivik Brassmere* — an aging gnome veteran with a steel left arm, monocle targeting lens, and a reputation for absolute punctuality.

The Brass Wardens are Lexovar's premier mercenary company, built primarily from remodeled, repaired, and outdated constructs no longer used in frontline national service. Rather than destroy valuable machines, Lexovar refitted them into a profitable contract force commanded by living officers, artificers, and engineers.

The Brass Wardens rely on constructs for most of their fighting force. Their humanoid members — mostly gnomes — serve as commanders and engineers rather than common soldiers. A Brass Warden formation may look like an army of machines, but living minds guide every advance. Without those officers and repair crews, the constructs are powerful tools without real judgment.

They specialize in rail security, vault protection, cargo escort, urban defense, siege support, and anti-bandit operations. Their ranks include brass sentinels, heavy shield units, repeater constructs, and utility machines maintained with strict efficiency. To the Wardens, every contract is part patrol, part engineering challenge, and part restoration project.

---

### The Obsidian Chain

*Primary Nation/Area: Lexovar*
*Symbol: A black chain on a book*
*Leader: High Seer Vaelthas* — rumored warlock of dark powers

A cult of secrecy and domination worshipping a forgotten god. Possible infiltration of magic schools is believed.

The Obsidian Chain is led by High Seer Vaelthas, a rumored warlock tied to dark powers. It is a cult of secrecy, domination, and forbidden knowledge. It gathers failed apprentices, ambitious mages, compromised officials, and scholars willing to trade conscience for power. Its structure is hierarchical and dangerous, with outer servants collecting secrets while inner circles control the deeper doctrine. Its goal is magical control through fear, ritual, forbidden texts, and infiltration. In Lexovar, it may appear as a secret study circle or hidden academy faction. Elsewhere, it may look like a cult cell, forbidden book trade, or patronage network offering power at a terrible price.

---

### The Golden Chain

*Primary Nation/Area: Lexovar*
*Symbol: A golden chain on a book*
*Leader: Valeke* — a charismatic dwarf rogue wielding a named crossbow called Beatrice

Sister group to the Obsidian Chain, focused on crime, extortion, and muscle.

The Golden Chain is the subtler half of the Chain's influence, focused on politics, leverage, bribery, and blackmail. Led by Valeke, a charismatic dwarf rogue who wields a named crossbow called Beatrice, the Golden Chain moves through respectable rooms more easily than dark shrines. It controls people through secrets, debts, favors, scandal, and access to restricted knowledge. Its structure is practical and careful, built from fixers, corrupt officials, informants, collectors, fences, and quiet enforcers who act only when soft pressure fails. Its goal is ownership of influence rather than worship of power. In Lexovar, it may look like private brokerage around laboratories and archives. In other regions, it may appear as political consultants, blackmailers, or wealthy patrons with dangerous files.

## Old Blood

### Gnomes — Cunning of the Old Blood

Gnomes who awaken the Old Blood develop a mysterious mark or tattoo around the eyes, believed to grant superior magical insight.

> **Level 7 Power:** Once per long rest, you can cast Identify or Dispel Magic as an action.
> **Drawback — Mental Strain:** After casting the spell, you take psychic damage equal to 3 × your proficiency bonus. This damage cannot be reduced or prevented.

## Grudges & Political Strain

Though gnomes are mostly peaceful, resentment from other nations festers.

- High rail taxes and restricted magitech access stir tension.
- Gnomes' dependence on trade due to lack of fragments is politically risky.
- Internal power struggles between magic school leaders are constant.

Even in a land of progress, secrets, sabotage, and political maneuvering keep every gnome glancing over their shoulder.

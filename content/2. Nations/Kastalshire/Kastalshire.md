# Kastalshire — Government, Military, Economy & Culture
*Humans of Eryndor*

---

## Government

The humans of Eryndor are governed by the Kingdom of Kastalshire, an absolute monarchy supported by a rigid feudal system, old noble bloodlines, and a powerful court centered in Contramara. The crown claims authority over the entire realm, but the lived strength of that authority depends heavily on distance, wealth, roads, rails, and local loyalty.

Kastalshire is ruled by the High King. Beneath the royal family are five duchies, each ruled by a duke. Each duchy contains two counties, each led by a count. Below the counts are barons, who typically hold towns and villages. Beneath the barons are lords and landed knights — minor nobles who hold manors, villages, strategic castles, bridge forts, mountain passes, mines, mills, watchtowers, or smaller tracts of land in exchange for military service and local protection.

Village mayors and leaders are not always nobles, though they may hold considerable local authority. They fall under the jurisdiction of their local Baron or landed Knight lord, a system often rife with corruption, favoritism, and patronage. In practice, a mayor near Contramara may have more influence than a poor landed knight in the frontier, while a respected village elder in the outer duchies may command more trust than the lord who technically rules them.

Most barons and higher nobles belong to Old Blood families or claim descent from such lines. This gives the upper nobility a sense of ancient legitimacy and inherited power. Landed knights may or may not possess Old Blood ancestry. Some are minor scions of powerful houses, while others are newly elevated families, military heroes, or descendants of once-greater lines now reduced to small holdings.

### The Crown and the Realm

The Palace of Kastal in Contramara is the royal seat, while the Citadel serves as the bureaucratic heart of the kingdom. From Contramara, the crown oversees taxes, military obligations, official records, courts, academy appointments, and the rail and road systems that bind the duchies to the capital.

The crown presents itself as the guardian of unity and the final protector of the **King's Peace**: the idea that roads, courts, markets, bridges, subjects, and lawful petitions all fall beneath royal protection. The closer one stands to Contramara, the more real and immediate this protection feels. In the outer duchies, the King's Peace may be invoked more often than it is enforced.

Kastalshire's central tension is not that the crown is weak. It is that the crown is strongest when everything flows through Contramara. The rails have strengthened the realm, but they have also made the capital the unavoidable center of trade. All rails run to Contramara, and from Contramara to the other duchies. Many nobles and the Crown prosper because of this arrangement. Others quietly resent it.

### Courtly and Frontier Nobility

Kastalshire's nobility is divided not only by rank, but by proximity to power. Courtiers near Contramara tend toward ceremony, tournaments, marriage politics, and legal maneuvering. Frontier nobles in the distant counties tend to be poorer, more martial, more traditional, and more dependent on local loyalty.

A good noble believes rank increases obligation. A corrupt noble believes rank confirms superiority. This moral divide is one of the defining tensions of Kastalshire. Some noble houses produce genuine protectors who see their titles as burdens before privileges. Others use the language of chivalry to disguise greed, negligence, or ambition.

## Military

Kastalshire possesses the most diverse military tradition in Eryndor. Though the Kingdom does not maintain a single massive central army in the manner of the Drakmir Dominion, it relies on noble retainers, county levies, castle garrisons, town guards, royal institutions, and military obligations spread across the feudal order.

Nevertheless, the human chivalric cavalry charge is legendary. The Knights of Kastalshire are among the most culturally celebrated warriors in the world, and the image of the armored knight riding beneath a house banner remains one of Kastalshire's most powerful symbols.

By royal law each Count must use 20 to 30 percent of their tax revenue on defense. This includes levy training, castle construction and maintenance, bridge defense, road patrols, tower upkeep, and local readiness. Dereliction of these duties is the most common reason for removal from power, though the seriousness of enforcement varies widely by wealth, political favor, and distance from Contramara.

### Military Unit: The Lance

The basic noble military unit is the Lance — armed, armored, and trained under the authority of the local count or the noble to whom the knight owes service.

- 1 knight
- 1 to 2 squires
- 2 to 3 archers
- 2 to 3 spearmen
- 1 to 2 camp followers (seamstress, cook, washer, groom, or other support worker)

A Lance is not merely a battlefield unit. In rural Kastalshire it may serve as road patrol, monster response, escort, tax guard, and visible proof that the local lord still remembers his obligations.

### Knight Errantry and Hedge Knights

Knight errantry is one of Kastalshire's oldest chivalric customs. At its purest, it is the practice of a knight riding without fixed station, serving the King's Peace wherever need is found. In the wealthy lands near Contramara, errantry is praised in song and mocked in practice. In the outer counties, it is often essential to survival.

**The Green Road** is the rite of passage for many newly titled knights. After receiving title, a young knight may spend a season, a year, or longer riding the roads before taking permanent service. The purpose is to prove that the knight's vows are more than courtly words. A Green Spur knight may escort travelers, defend villages, hunt monsters, answer petitions, or serve as champion for those without one.

**The Penance Road** is errantry imposed after poor judgment, cruelty, cowardice, abuse of authority, failure, or shame. A knight may be ordered to serve poor villages, guard a dangerous road, protect widows and orphans, escort pilgrims, surrender tournament winnings, ride without heraldic display, or accept no noble hospitality until the vow is fulfilled. The best return humbled. The worst never really go.

**The Hedge Road** is the life of the landless or unattached knight. Hedge knights may be poor nobles, bastards, ruined veterans, displaced heirs, disgraced retainers, or idealists who refuse permanent service. Landed knights guard a place. Hedge knights guard the road.

In counties such as Caer Valance, Lyraon, Arestead, Raethalor, and Varonhold, hedge knights are cherished. They may be poor, road-worn, and patched together, but they are often the only trained warriors who arrive before danger does. Near Contramara, such knights are often sneered at as vagabonds, failed nobles, a disgrace for true knights. Kastalshire officially honors knight errantry as sacred, but socially Contramara often humiliates those who remain errant too long.

## Economy

The human economy is broad and adaptable. Subsistence farmers sustain the population. Craftsmen and artisans handle mass production and transportation inside the kingdom. Miners, foresters, sailors, hunters, fishermen, millers, wagoners, and guild laborers make the kingdom function from its smallest villages to its royal capital.

Over the last fifty years, magitech development has transformed Kastalshire's agriculture and quietly reshaped the kingdom's population. Fragment-powered irrigation pumps, vermin wards, soil-enrichment, enchanted plows, grain-preserving storehouses, and rail-assisted food transport have allowed farms to produce larger and more reliable harvests than previous generations thought possible. These advances have been most visible in the richer central and southern duchies, where noble estates and merchant-backed guilds can afford new tools, but even rural regions have benefited from improved mills, safer storage, and faster movement of grain during shortage years.

As crop yields have risen, famine has become less common, infant survival has improved, towns have grown, and families have been able to support more children. This growth has strengthened Kastalshire's economy and military levies, but it has also increased pressure on land, inheritance, roads, and local governance, feeding the kingdom's ambition while deepening the strain between wealthy, well-supported regions and the poorer outer reaches.

The population boom has also increased the number of young men and women seeking admission to the Praetorian. For many families with too many heirs and too little land, Praetorian service offers food, pay, status, and a path into the machinery of the kingdom. At the same time, crowded towns, strained roads, land disputes, and displaced laborers have given criminal organizations more room to grow. None has benefited more than the Brotherhood of the Rats.

Kastalshire primarily trades with Halflings, Gnomes, and Dwarves, but maintains workable relations with most nations when practical need requires it. Human merchants are rarely the absolute masters of any one field, but they are persistent, flexible, and quick to absorb useful practices from other cultures.

The merchant class is organized through guilds. These guilds regulate trades, train apprentices, protect contracts, negotiate with nobles, fund public works, and give ambitious commoners a collective voice in a kingdom dominated by blood and title. A lone merchant can be ignored. A guild can bargain with a count. A coalition of guilds can force even the crown to listen.

Guilds do not replace feudal authority. They operate inside it, around it, and sometimes against it. Wealthy guild families may buy property, sponsor temples, fund roads, and arrange marriages with poorer noble houses. This creates constant tension between money and birth.

## Fragment Access

Kastalshire has access to several modest Fragment mines and several magitech laboratories, though nothing on the gnomish scale. Its fragments support military development, limited battle magic, infrastructure, experimental transport, and noble or royal projects.

Human fragment use tends to be practical and institutional. Where gnomes innovate obsessively and dwarves mine at immense scale, Kastalshire adapts fragments into existing systems: farming, roads, defensive works, and experimental projects meant to strengthen the crown.

## Culture

Kastalshire is a feudal society shaped by noble ideals under constant pressure. Its people believe in chivalry, ambition, just life and law. Those ideals are real, but they are unevenly lived. At its best, Kastalshire produces brave knights, honest merchants, and common people who endure with dignity. At its worst, it gives corrupt people beautiful words to hide selfish action.

Chivalry is enforced through a rigid caste structure. Commoners rarely rise in status except through military heroism, temple service, or extraordinary royal favor. Even then, their ascent is watched carefully by those who inherited what others must earn.

The closer one stands to Contramara, the more chivalry becomes a matter of convenience and ceremony. In the furthest reaches of the kingdom, survival depends on chivalry, neighborly care, and the defense of the defenseless.

### The Three Ideals

Kastalshire culture can be understood as three overlapping ideals: chivalry, ambition, and just life under law. These ideals are associated with different classes, but the lines blur constantly.

**Chivalry** is the noble ideal. Good nobles believe power exists to protect the weak, defend the realm, and answer the needs of those beneath them. Corrupt nobles use chivalry as theater, treating honor as reputation rather than responsibility.

**Ambition** is the ideal of most merchant guilds. Good ambition builds businesses, elevates families, and creates something lasting. Corrupt ambition exploits labor, buys silence, and calls greed progress.

**Just life and law** is the common ideal of the common peasant. They want fair taxes, safe roads, honest weights, real protection, just courts that hear the poor, and lords who answer petitions. To the common folk, order is good when it protects peaceful life.

These ideals often cross class lines. Poor nobles may think like merchants. Wealthy merchants may imitate nobles. A blacksmith, reeve, priest, hedge knight, or guildmaster may live at the crossroads of all three.

### Caste and Outcast

**"The Caste and the Outcast"** is a frontier phrase. It describes the divide between those close enough to Contramara to receive royal attention and those too far away to depend on it.

**The Caste** live near power. They have courts that answer quickly, roads that stay guarded, and nobles the crown actually hears.

**The Outcast** live beyond dependable protection — the frontier counties and towns whose lords remember them only when taxes are due.

### Seasonal Courts

Justice in Kastalshire is tied to the agricultural year. Ruling nobles are expected to hear cases during **Planting Court**, **Harvest Court**, and **Yule Court**. These courts allow commoners to bring disputes, debts, petitions, inheritance claims, tax complaints, land arguments, and pleas for mercy before those who rule them.

Good nobles treat these courts as sacred duties. Corrupt nobles use them as public theater. No noble in authority cancels their court. In poorer counties, a seasonal court may be the only time common folk believe anyone above them is forced to listen.

### Human Adaptability

Humans are adaptable, but not because they are rootless. Kastalshire adapts by absorbing useful systems and turning them into institutions. This adaptability is one of Kastalshire's greatest strengths and one of its greatest dangers. The kingdom can reform without admitting defeat, but it has already centralized dangerously.

## Religion

Humans worship many gods. They have the broadest and most diverse set of religions in Eryndor, but the most revered are Helm, god of protection, and Lathander, god of renewal and light.

**Helm** is deeply respected in Kastalshire because his faith speaks to the kingdom's defensive soul. His doctrines of vigilance, duty, and protection fit the watchtower, the shield wall, and the knightly oath. Guards pray to Helm before taking post. Frontier villages invoke him when danger gathers beyond the lantern light. Military households honor him as the god who teaches that peace survives only when someone is willing to keep watch.

**Lathander** is beloved because his faith speaks to human hope and renewal. His doctrines of dawn, growth, and new beginnings fit the farmer's field, the young knight's vow, and the builder's dream. Farmers pray to him over seed and harvest. Healers call on him when life hangs by a thread. Reformers and ambitious families honor him as the god who teaches that broken things can rise again.

## Mercenaries & Criminal Organizations

### Iron-Banded Mercenaries

The Iron-Banded Mercenaries are a royally chartered company. Members wear a distinctive iron band on their person at all times, which is where they get their name.

The company was formed to combat corruption and respond to wars, particularly against the Goblinoids and threats from the sea. Criminals and debtors can be sentenced to service for a minimum of ten years, with all crimes and debts forgiven upon completion. Escape, or aiding an escapee, is punishable by immediate execution.

### Radiant Vanguard

The Radiant Vanguard is a Lawful Good order based in Aremir, known as Champions of Justice and Protectors of the Innocent.

The order is led by **Sir Aelric Dawnshield**, a golden-armored paladin with a glowing sword. Aelric left a corrupt holy order to found this righteous organization.

### Brotherhood of the Rat

The Brotherhood of the Rat is a criminal network spread across Kastalshire, controlling slums, ports, underground economies, and vulnerable communities. Known for extortion, mugging, illegal gambling, smuggling, and protection rackets.

The Rats are known for the use of a thin dagger called the **rat's tail**. Their leader is **Creeve Barrow**, a brutal enforcer with a knack for puncturing lungs and breaking bones.

## Old Blood

Some humans awaken ancient power known as the Old Blood. Those who do are immediately elevated to minor nobility, provided they have noble ancestry within five generations.

> **Level 7 Power:** Once per long rest, when you fail an attack roll, saving throw, or ability check, you may reroll it.
> **Drawback:** After using this feature, you immediately gain 1 level of exhaustion.

In Kastalshire, Old Blood reinforces the nobility's claim to ancient legitimacy. Most barons and higher nobles are connected to Old Blood families or claim descent from them. This does not make all nobles worthy, but it does make bloodline politics difficult to dismiss. To many commoners, Old Blood is both proof of destiny and proof that the powerful will always find another reason to remain powerful.

## Grudges & External Political Strain

Human expansionism causes anxiety among neighboring nations. Longer-lived races find human short-term decisions difficult to manage, while humans often view longer-lived peoples as manipulative, slow, or condescending.

Relations with elves are especially strained. Humans accuse them of centuries-long manipulation, an accusation rooted in the old influence of elven emissaries, the burning of the library of Caer Thian, and the reforms of King Gregor IV.

Ongoing hostilities with the Redmarch Coalition keep tensions high. Pirates and Goliath raids remain sources of bloodshed, both by and against the humans. Coastal defense, frontier raids, monster threats, criminal infiltration, and the question of crown authority all continue to shape Kastalshire's foreign and domestic posture.

## Notable NPCs

### High King Vaerin Thalros of Kastalshire

**Title:** High King of Kastalshire, Lord of the Citadel, Hammer of the Southern March
**Seat:** The Palace of Kastal, [[Contramara]] | **Age:** 59 | **Race:** Human

Commanding and cunning. King Vaerin sees the world as a chess board. Every noble owes him loyalty, every town owes him taxes, and every duchy owes him blood. He is not cruel, but he has no patience for weakness or sentimentality. He favors his son Dorian for succession.

**Appearance:** Tall and severe, with long iron-grey hair and a neatly trimmed beard. His armor is always immaculate, inscribed with the sigil of House Thalros: a lion with a broken rose in its jaws. His eyes are steel blue and unreadable.

**Crown Relationship with the Dukes:**
- Duke [[Garran Velmouth|Velmouth]] of Aremir: Tense. [[Garran Velmouth|Velmouth]] distrusts the crown and is closely watched by Dorian.
- Duke Hawnspear of Caer Thian: Respectful. Seen as loyal, but expendable if he cannot control piracy.
- Duchess Varron of Lyricast: Loyal but politically minor. [[Kaelen Thalros|Kaelen]] uses this duchy as a playground.
- Duke Renward of Rath: Feared and quiet. The king believes Rath's isolation makes it ripe for takeover.
- Duke Vael of Varondale: Family by marriage. Strained by rumors that the queen protects her Varondale kin over the crown.

### Queen Aralyn Thalros

Formerly [[Aralyn Thalros|Aralyn]] of Varondale, daughter of the late Duke of [[Varonhold]]. Wed to Vaerin in a political marriage that ended a brewing succession crisis and sealed Varondale's loyalty to the crown.

Calculated and observant. [[Aralyn Thalros|Aralyn]] is politically brilliant and handles much of the realm's soft power. She controls court gossip, organizes diplomatic functions, and is known for identifying threats before her husband sees them. Regal and admired, but whispered to be manipulative behind closed doors. She favors her son [[Kaelen Thalros|Kaelen]] for succession.

Whispers suggest she still maintains private correspondence with her younger brother, now Count of [[Hearthpoint]], who has been more resistant to the crown's taxes.

### The Twin Heirs: Prince Dorian and Prince Kaelen Thalros

**Age:** 22. Recently knighted. Both serve in high positions within the Praetorian Order and are seen across the duchies on official missions.

**Prince [[Dorian Thalros]], The Iron Spear**
Stoic and loyal with a commander's mind. A military prodigy trained by senior captains of Caer Thian and Aremir. He leads inspections of militia training and regional levies, currently stationed in [[Falbrin]], quietly evaluating Duke [[Garran Velmouth|Velmouth]]'s loyalty. Rumored to be promised in marriage to a noblewoman of House Dallinar.

**Prince [[Kaelen Thalros|Kaelen]] Thalros, The Silver Tongue**
Charismatic, clever, and ambitious. Studies diplomacy and arcane history at The Arcanum. Travels frequently between Lyricast and Rath, courting favor among counts and manipulating courtly politics. Rumored to support annexing Caer Thian's coast as a crown-controlled province if piracy cannot be curbed.

The twins are extremely close but often take differing approaches: Dorian through force and loyalty, [[Kaelen Thalros|Kaelen]] through charm and intrigue. They are rumored to both love the same woman, though her identity remains a mystery.
